## Why the CT is there

A radiotherapy plan needs two kinds of image. Soft tissue tells the clinician where the target is. Electron density tells the dose engine how the beam will deposit energy. CT has always supplied the second. MRI is better at the first.

When both scanners are available, the two images are registered and the plan is built on the pair. When the CT is missing, delayed, or hard to reach, that pairing becomes a bottleneck.

## What synthetic CT is asking

MRI-only workflows ask whether a CT-like image can be generated from MR data with enough fidelity that the dose calculation still holds. The generated image is not a photograph of bone. It is an estimate of the density map the algorithm needs.

The physics questions are concrete: how much error is acceptable in the Hounsfield units, where does the estimate fail near air and metal, and how is that error checked before a patient is treated.

## Access is part of the physics

In settings where a CT slot is scarce, or where a second appointment is a day's travel, removing that scan is not a convenience. It is a way to keep the treatment on the MRI that already showed the anatomy.
