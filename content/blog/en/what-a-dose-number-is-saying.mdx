## A number is a method

Dose is reported as if it were a reading on a scale. Behind it sits a protocol: the quantity, the phantom or patient model, the instrument, the calibration, and the uncertainty. Change any of those and the same display can mean something else.

Radiation protection begins by asking which of those choices produced the figure you are looking at.

## Images carry dose too

Diagnostic imaging is often discussed as if it were free of the obligations that sit around therapy. It is not. Every CT acquisition is a negotiation between image quality and the energy deposited in tissue.

The medical physicist is there to keep that negotiation honest: enough information to decide, not more dose than the decision needs.

## Teaching the uncertainty

Students should leave a course able to say "this number is useful" and also "this number is silent about X." That second sentence is where safety lives.
