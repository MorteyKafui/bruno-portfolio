## Measurement before vocabulary

Students can recite the definition of absorbed dose and still not feel what a gray means beside a patient. The first job of teaching is to make the quantity tactile: what was measured, with what, and what would have been different if the instrument had been wrong.

That habit is the same one a medical physicist takes into quality assurance. The classroom is where it should start.

## Questions that survive the clinic

A useful lecture ends with a question the student can still ask on a ward: is this image showing anatomy or an artefact. Is this dose justified. Who is responsible if the number is off.

Those questions travel better than a slide deck of formulae.

## What the next generation inherits

Physics education in this setting is not only about producing physicists. It is about producing teachers and clinicians who can explain the same idea twice: once to a peer, and once to someone whose care depends on it.
